Lawns

Hello, I am very passionate about nature and our effects on it, and in this site I talk about one of the largest causes of habitat destruction and biodiversity loss that no one mentions: the lawn.

Why lawns?

Many people who live in the suburbs are inclined to believe that where they live is closer to nature. Not like those pesky city dwellers that are surrounded by cement and smog. No, we live in the suburbs. Green, short grass as far as the eye can see. So natural! What an escape from the lifelessness of the city!!

WRONG!!!

Lawns are possibly the furthest you could get to true nature while also technically supporting life. One type of life, specifically.

Kentucky bluegrass Poa pratensis is a plant native to Europe, northern Asia and parts of northern Africa. It, as well as Bermuda Grass Cynodon dactylon and St. Augustinegrass Stenotaphrum secundatum are what most people think of as turfgrass. Now, these plants are not native to North America, but that is a whole other problem in itself. What I want to focus on, is how you find them in their natural range. In the wild, you do not see these neatly cut. You do not see them green year-round. And most crucially, you do not see them growing in a monoculture, or without insects.

What is the problem with this?

Lawns and nature

Almost all environments on Earth require multiple organisms (and even abiotic factors too, like the climate!) working in harmony to keep it healthy. Even taking away or changing just one of these factors can lead to environmental detriment. It is the reason why erosion exploded in Yellowstone after wolves were exterminated: without wolves regulating the elk population their numbers started to skyrocket, leading to overgrazing of grasses which help stabilize the soil with their roots and causing erosion (this, coincidentally, was also the main cause of the 1930's dust bowl) as well as delcine of native plant species [1]. This balance is the same in the native range of our three dear grasses - caterpillars and other insects hide within and eat the leaves of the grass, which can later be eaten by birds which get eaten by bigger birds.

In an 'environment' like a lawn, no food web exists. Just one type of plant is completely unable to support a diverse ecosystem, let alone an ecosystem at all. Many animals like caterpillars, fireflies and birds rely on a diet of native plants to sustain themselves. A lawn does nothing to support them. Even generalists like bees will not gain anything from a lawn, as there is nothing to be pollinated. Even the animals that are fine eating grass, like geese, might not be able to properly consume it because it's doused in pesticides.

Not that people like geese anyway. But if you're gonna keep massive unused fields full of the stuff what do you expect?

A small tangent...

I know that a lot of people will justify us keeping lawns for the sake of sports. Oh no, where will all the kids go if we replace some of the grass in the park with flowers? Where will people hold their grass-only picnics? OH NO! WHAT WILL HAPPEN THEN! THINK OF THE KIDS!!!

While grass is definitely needed for playing fields, there is absolutely no reason why an entire city has to be covered in them. I'm not talking about just parks, I mean the gutters along roads, the business park, the clear zones along highways. There is no reason why we should put grass in these places. It strips us of the only places in the city where nature can live without people getting mad. It makes our cities more bland than they already were. They cost us more in labor because a guy has to come out there every week to mow it and keep it pristine. For a place no one will visit, no one will walk through and no one will remember.

Is there truly any reason why we have to keep doing this? Really?

How do people prefer the useless green cement over this? A little nature wouldn't hurt you. At least, not as much as the glyphosate from Roundup.

Continuing...

Our obsession with keeping a pristine lawn devoid of 'weeds' (I hate this term so much) completely bars us from being able to sustain life in our yards. Not just that you're adding new (probably native!) plants to your property, but also the plethora of insects, birds and other life you will be helping as well.

This is only made worse by the fact we must keep our lawns under two inches tall at all times. Not even mentioning the air and noise pollution lawnmowers cause [2], having such short grass means insects are unable to live there. Now, I get that a lot of people find insects 'icky' and should not belong in neighborhoods. This is also a whole other problem in nature and entomology spheres and is not the topic of this page. What I will say at least, is that insects are the backbone of most ecosystems, and killing them all just because you find them scary will lead to a complete collapse of your local environment. Be normal about them.

Why I care (and what you can do)

Habitat loss is one of the leading causes of biodiversity loss in the world. Over the past five decades, population of birds in the United States has collapsed over 70% [3]. In five decades. This problem is serious and if we do not do anything about it, we might lose these species forever. I know a lot of people care about this issue, but many just have no idea what to do about it. Removing the lawn is one of the easiest - planting native supports your local ecosystem, gives back land to nature, has no need for pesticides and is generally just nicer to look at.

Because personally, I'd prefer actual nature over an astroturf abyss.
